Research Motivation

The questions I want to understand

Human Mobility

How do everyday constraints shape the journeys people make?

My own school commutes and later move to Beijing made travel time and accessibility tangible questions. I want to understand how residence, work schedules, school obligations and household responsibilities organise daily movement, and how these individual decisions contribute to congestion and unequal access to opportunities.

Activity Chains

What can incomplete observations tell us about daily activities?

Working with coarse mobile phone signalling records led me to ask what we can learn about activities when locations and destinations remain uncertain. I aim to infer activity purposes and recurring structures across days while preserving uncertainty, individual variation and the coordination between household members. I also want to examine which activity information can be retained without reconstructing precise individual movements.

GeoAI & Site Selection

How can geographic evidence support better location decisions?

Public-space assessment and mapping applications made me interested in how places function within people's daily lives. I aim to combine mobility observations, POIs, imagery and transport networks to evaluate accessibility, commercial catchments and service gaps. Differences in data availability and quality motivate me to examine how these analyses remain reliable across cities, especially where direct mobility observations are scarce.

Master's thesis · 2025–2026

Activity Semantics and School-Related Travel Chains

I examine how school journeys and work commutes are coordinated within household activity chains, including how later commitments constrain earlier journeys. Stop timing, duration, recurrence and sequence position inform Bayesian activity inference. Month-long mobility profiles capture overlapping behavioural tendencies and dependencies between daily activities.
